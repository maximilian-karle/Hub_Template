## 1. Core idea

Three to five sentences in our own words: what problem the framework
solves and the one idea that makes it work. No paraphrase of a single book.

## 2. When to use, and when not

- **Use when:** the situation or question it fits
- **Do not use when:** where it breaks, or where a simpler tool is enough

## 3. The model

The framework itself, as a table, matrix or flow. This is the artefact that
gets copied into a project.

## 4. How to apply

Numbered steps, each with its input, its output, and who has to be in the
room. Name the artefact every step leaves behind.

## 5. Own adaptation

What differs from the sources and why. This section is what separates a
framework in this folder from the literature it draws on.

## 8. Limits and open questions

What is untested, what the sources contradict, what we have not read yet.

## 9. Interview pitch

Two or three sentences: how to explain the framework and the case it was
used on in a consulting interview. Leave it empty while the status is
"In trial". A pitch without a case behind it does not hold up in an interview.
